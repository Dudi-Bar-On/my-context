## 2. The principle, as applied

Stated in the task and used exactly as given, one rule throughout rather than argued fresh per
task:

- **IN** — work without which the product misleads a reader, loses data, or leaves a visible
  seam where something is half built.
- **OUT** — an enhancement; a second surface for something that already works once; a
  consolidation or DRY refactor of things that each already function; internal process, gate or
  test hygiene no reader of the shipped product ever sees; anything resting on taste the owner
  has not ruled on.
- **ASK** — the task's own body says a ruling is still owed, or the principle genuinely cannot
  place it without guessing. Not guessed at.

Three refinements, made explicit because each decided several calls and a silent choice there
would be exactly the guess this document must not make:

1. **"A reader" includes the CLI and MCP surfaces, not only the browser.** A refusal message that
   hides its own escape hatch, or a tool schema missing a field its sibling tool has, misleads a
   reader of that surface the same way a wrong sentence on a screen would.
2. **i18n breakage counts as misleading.** EN/HE parity is a stated, tested requirement of this
   product (`strings-parity.test.ts`, the mockup's own bilingual design). English text on the
   Hebrew screen, or a Hebrew translation that says the opposite of the English, is the product
   misleading a reader in the reader's own language — IN, not an enhancement. Likewise, a
   silently-inert or silently-stale control (a cap that stops applying with no sentence saying
   so, a dash standing in for "never measured" indistinguishable from "measured as none") trains
   the reader to trust a number the product cannot vouch for — the same pattern
   `STD-a-measured-zero-is-drawn-and-named-an-unmeasured-thing-is` already treats as serious, so
   it is treated the same way here even where nothing on screen is literally false.
3. **An owner ruling already on record does not, by itself, make a task IN.** Several tasks carry
   an owner ruling (`categories/24`, `categories/25`, `hooks/33`, `ui3/15`). Each was placed by
   asking whether the product is worse *today* without it, not by the ruling's existence alone.
   Most land IN because the ruling exists precisely because something was missing or wrong.
   `ui3/15` (typed SQL on Ask) is the one placed OUT despite a direct owner ruling: it adds a
   genuinely new, second query surface, gated behind two unbuilt prerequisites (`ctx.api` has no
   `POST`; the read-only guard has a known false positive) — additive capability, not a fix for
   something misleading today. Being ruled-on answers "should this ever be built," which is a
   different question from the one this cut answers: "does v2.0 need it."
