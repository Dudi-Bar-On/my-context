Which icon set survives a 12.5px monospace row, read for hours, in two directions?

Eight candidates, the same six action glyphs — refresh, copy, open, confirm, search, add — rendered at 14px and 16px, on the real dark‑tinted glass pane over the real purple/teal ground, using the exact tokens from docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-21-web-ui-visual-direction-design.md §2. No category glyphs are tested — that call is already made (§6) — and the three tier marks (circle, square, diamond) are drawn shapes per that ruling, not pulled from any library.

Every SVG below came from a package actually installed today (npm install, not memory) and is inlined verbatim except where a caption says otherwise. Full provenance is in RATIONALE.md beside this file.

LTR / RTL

Of the six, only open mirrors — it depicts an arrow escaping a box toward the reading‑end corner, so the corner has to flip with the direction. Refresh, copy, confirm, search and add stay put: none of them encode a spatial "forward"; a cyclical‑motion glyph, a duplicate‑document glyph, a checkmark, a magnifying glass and a plus sign don't have a reading direction to get wrong. None of the eight libraries ship a pre‑mirrored variant or direction metadata for these six — mirroring here is one CSS rule, [dir="rtl"] .ic-mirror{transform:scaleX(-1)}, applied only to open.

forced-colors: active

Verified today against Chromium 152 (headless, CDP Emulation.setEmulatedMedia with forced-colors: active) on this exact glass pane — the spec's own open question §8 called this "untested against glass." The measured result: both of the pane's background‑image layers (gloss and tint) are forced to none; background-color, border-color and inherited text color are forced to system colours; backdrop-filter is not forced and keeps blurring, now with nothing coloured behind it. The glass material does not survive forced‑colors as a material — it degrades to a blurred system‑coloured box, which is the honest baseline every icon here is judged against below.

For the icons specifically: Chromium's own UA stylesheet sets svg { forced-color-adjust: preserve-parent-color } (confirmed in third_party/blink/renderer/core/css/svg.css, matching the CSS Color Adjustment spec, and re‑confirmed here with two more direct measurements). In practice that means an icon using fill="currentColor", inheriting color normally from its container, tracks whatever forced colour that container receives — measured: rgb(139,156,230) becomes forced black the moment its wrapper is forced, automatically. That's every candidate above, unpatched originals excepted. A hardcoded literal fill — fill="#8b9ce6", or simply no fill attribute at all, which defaults to opaque black — is left completely alone, in every theme, measured at rgb(139,156,230) and rgb(0,0,0) respectively, forced or not. That second case is exactly what Fluent and Material Symbols ship unpatched: a colour that never adapts is not a safety net, it's a coin flip — it happens to read fine forced‑to‑white‑on‑black here purely because black already contrasts against a light forced background, and would vanish outright against a dark forced background. The currentColor patch is not a cosmetic nicety; it's what lets an icon participate in forced‑colors at all.
