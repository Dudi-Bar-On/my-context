Injection preview

✅ exactly what Claude gets — including when that is nothing

The block Claude receives at the start of a session in this project. Right now the hook sends no block at all, because nothing is eligible to send. That is the true state of this repository, and it is what most people believe is not true of theirs.

Four bars, four tiers, and at zero items the structure is the only thing on the screen — which makes this the best explanation of the tiers the product will ever get to give.

Coverage gaps

✅ names what is missing, which no listing can

Gaps are the difference between what you have covered and what you have not. With nothing covered there is no difference — so at zero this screen shows the half that is still true and still useful: which of the 21 categories you have nothing in, and what each one is for.

Normative — 13 categories · injected · 0 items

These steer future work, so they are the ones Claude reads. When Claude captures one it lands as a draft and governs nothing until you promote it. When you capture one it is active at once.

constraint · rule · requirement · invariant · standard · pattern · glossary · instruction · non_goal · open_question · runbook · environment · known_issue

Rationale — 8 categories · never injected · 0 items

History you can search, and Claude can look up deliberately. Never injected — which is why an agent's capture of one lands active immediately: it cannot steer anything.

adr · decision · lesson · tradeoff · assumption · edge_case · risk · reference

known_issue sits on the normative side even though it reads like a fact: its whole job is “this is broken, do not spend effort here”, and it cannot do that job from a tier Claude never reads.

empty corpus Pick the category first; it is the one thing you cannot change later.

type is fixed at creation and there is no retype — a duplicate filed under the wrong category can only be superseded. The category also decides the tier, which decides whether the item is ever injected at all.

This writes, and without --yes it will refuse and tell you the item would govern at once.

Budget simulator

✅ drag the budget, watch what fits

Nothing can spill out of an empty corpus, so there is nothing to drag. The screen still has one true thing to say, and it is the thing that matters before you have paid for it.

Your budgets, as configured

Units are estimateTokens — characters ÷ 4, an approximation with error in both directions and not a bound. The screen says so rather than implying a precision the estimate does not have.

empty corpus An item that does not fit spills whole, and is named.

Never truncated, never dropped in silence. This screen becomes the fastest way to answer “what is my budget costing me” at around 40 items; today it would have to invent items to show you anything, and inventing them is how a demo teaches a false number.

Why isn't this here?

✅ one binding reason, not a checklist of suspects

RULE-every-price-is-an-integer-of-minor-units against src/billing/charge.ts, event tool. The selector applies its filters in a fixed order, so there is always exactly one first reason — and naming the first is honest where listing all six is noise.

1 ✓eligible active, not retired, not superseded, category enabled, not past valid_until
2 ✓normative tier rule is normative, so it is injectable at all
3 ✓focus no focus is set, so nothing is narrowed away
4 ✗scope This is the reason. Its scope is src/payments/**, which matches 0 files in this repository. src/ matches 412 and src/billing/ matches 38 — there is no src/payments/ here. doctor reports this as dead_scope.
5seen not reached — already-delivered items are filtered before budgeting
6budget not reached — an item that gets here and does not fit spills whole, with its reason
mycontext edit RULE-every-price-is-an-integer-of-minor-units --scope "src/billing/**" --yes

This writes. Composed here, run by you — the page never edits an item.

One component, three jobs. The same ladder answers the spill list on the preview, the “governs nothing” line under a stale draft, and every “I captured it and nothing happened” support question. It re-implements no rule: rungs 1–4 are injection(), rung 5 is the seen file, rung 6 is select()'s own spill reason.

Review queue

✅ the diff is the capability; the approval is a paste

Empty is the good state here, and the screen says so — otherwise a new user reads a blank panel as something they failed to find.

empty result Nothing is waiting. That is the healthy state, not a missing feature.

Anything Claude captures in one of the 13 normative categories lands here as a draft and governs nothing until you promote it. Rationale captures never appear here — they are never injected, so they cannot steer anything.

Drafts piling up unread is the failure this design invites: Claude keeps proposing, nothing governs, and you pay the cost of the tool with none of its benefit. When this number is not zero it is the only badge in the rail that turns amber.

The fastest way to have something here is mycontext ingest — every candidate it extracts from a document you already wrote is filed as a draft, so a new user meets this gate on their own prose before an agent ever tests it on them.

Configure

✅ shows what a change would do before it is made

The only screen with a complete corpus on day one, because its subject is config.json, which init already wrote. Its impact column is the empty half — and “affects 0 items today, and everything from now on” is the most useful sentence a new user can read, because these are the settings that are cheap to choose now and expensive to change later.

watchedDocs 3 globs docs/superpowers/specs/** · docs/superpowers/plans/** · docs/prd/** — 0 of the 61 documents in this repo match any of them.
empty result Nothing in this repository is being watched.

watchedDocs decides when Claude is nudged to capture after editing a document. The three defaults are this plugin author's own paths; your docs live in docs/adr/, docs/runbooks/ and CLAUDE.md. The list replaces, it does not merge, so these are the globs that match your tree:

"watchedDocs": ["docs/adr/**", "docs/runbooks/**", "CLAUDE.md"]

Paste into .my_context/config.json yourself. This page composes the diff and never writes the file — the deny hook already declares this file yours to change, and a UI that wrote it would be arguing with a rule the product enforces against its own agent.
