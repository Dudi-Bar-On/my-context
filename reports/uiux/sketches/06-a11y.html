Accessibility & bidi sketch

Four demonstrations. Toggle the language and the theme; everything below is meant to be inspected with a screen reader and with the mouse unplugged.

Screens

    1 — Bidi isolation, three ways

    Switch to Hebrew. The first block is the mockup's own Hebrew string, rendered the way the mockup renders it. The second and third are the same sentence with the technical runs isolated.

    Unisolated — what ships today

    The parentheses of injection() are neutrals sitting between a Latin run and Hebrew text. Rule N2 of the bidi algorithm gives them the paragraph direction, so they resolve RTL, get mirrored, and jump to the other side of the identifier. The ** of a glob and the -- of a flag do the same thing.

    Isolated with <bdi> and .m

    Built by substituting nodes into a placeholder string — {fn}, {glob} — never by concatenation and never through innerHTML. The isolate makes each run a single neutral object to the paragraph, so the runs sit in RTL reading order: first run rightmost. That is correct, and it is the thing most often "fixed" into a bug.

    Numbers, dates and plurals

    Native Intl output for the current language — no library, no polyfill.

    2 — Coverage tree

    One tab stop. ↑↓ move, →← expand and collapse (swapped under RTL), Home/End jump, * expands every sibling, typing letters jumps by name, Enter selects. Depth is aria-level and a logical padding-inline-start, never non-breaking spaces.

      3 — What governs

      Item Tier Why it applies Tokens

      The wrapper is role="region" with tabindex="0" and a name: a scrolling box that cannot be focused is unreachable to a keyboard user, and the mockup has four of them.

      4 — Audit stream

      Rows are appended, never re-rendered. The polite region announces a coalesced count on a 1.5 s timer, so a burst of forty records is one utterance rather than forty. "Announce every record" is the opt-in for people who want the firehose, and the pause button is WCAG 2.2.2, not politeness.
