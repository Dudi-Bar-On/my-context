The zero problem log(0) = −∞

Why "never" is a bucket and not a big number

A log axis cannot show zero, and it cannot show "did not happen" either. The standard fix is symlog — a linear region around zero, logarithmic outside it — and it is the wrong fix here. Symlog puts "never injected" on the same axis as "injected 47 sessions ago", which says never is the far end of a continuum. It is not: it is a different kind, and an item that has never been injected is a selection question, not a decay question. So the axis ends, a rule closes it, and the never bucket sits outside with its own terminal box and a square mark instead of a disc. The reader cannot accidentally read a distance between them, because there is not one.

Relations

an ego-graph, not a hairball

One focused item, radius 1, deterministic layered layout, hard cap of 60 nodes with an explicit "+N more". No physics, no dependency — and no physics is a design decision rather than a saving: a force layout puts the same corpus in a different place every time you open it, which is the one thing a ledger must never do.

What points at this, and what it points at

radius 1

Change the focus and the nodes travel to their new columns while the edges redraw under them. A graph that repaints instantly forces the reader to find their bearings again from scratch; a graph that moves lets them follow one node across the change, which is the only reason this view animates. What this page argues, and what it costs. Every number below was measured on this file, in this browser, on this machine.

The claim

Eighteen views, one instrument

A charting library draws the eleven charts every product already has. It does not draw a ghost lane, an absent tier, a never bucket, a not-examined hatch or a not-recorded void — and those five marks are the only ones this product could not buy. So the money goes into the substrate: one scale/tick/projection kernel, one hover-and-focus contract, one motion contract, one degradation contract, one table twin. Eighteen views stop being eighteen drawings and become eighteen configurations, and the marks that matter stay hand-written because they are the product.

The axis decision

What mirrors in Hebrew, and what does not

The mockup mirrors every chart by projection — X(u,W) flips each x-coordinate and ANC() flips each anchor, so the drawing mirrors while the glyphs stay upright. That machinery is right and this page keeps it. What this page changes is what it is applied to. Layout mirrors; a magnitude axis does not. Row labels, legends, tooltips, the never bucket and the whole reading order swap sides. But a numeric axis keeps its numbers growing away from the origin in the direction the numerals themselves are written, because Hebrew and Arabic mathematics both set notation left-to-right — the axis in a Hebrew textbook runs the same way it does in an English one. Mirroring it puts "50 sessions ago" where the eye expects "1". The control in the top bar switches between the two so this can be ruled on rather than assumed; the published survey of real RTL charts is genuinely split — roughly 58% keep the y-axis on the reading-start side and 33% mirror both — which is exactly why it belongs on a panel and not in a stylesheet.

The question for the owner: in Hebrew, does a magnitude axis mirror with the layout, or stay left-to-right with the numerals? This page ships the second answer and flips to the first in one attribute.
