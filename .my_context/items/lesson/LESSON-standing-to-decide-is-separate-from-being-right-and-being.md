# Standing to decide is separate from being right, and being right hides the overstep

**Standing to decide is separate from reaching the right answer. Having the
second does not give you the first.**

A reconciliation pass found four features that a specification described and the
design did not show. It removed or reduced all four and recorded them as
corrections. When the owner reviewed them, **two rulings went the other way and
two confirmed what the pass had done.**

The two it got right are the instructive half. Nothing about them looked wrong —
they were well reasoned, the outcome was correct, and had the owner not been
shown the list they would have passed unnoticed forever. **That is exactly what
makes overstepping dangerous: it is invisible in proportion to how good the
judgement was.** A wrong ruling gets caught. A right one taken without standing
becomes precedent.

**The distinction that matters, and it is not about confidence.** Two things
disagreeing is a reconciliation and needs no permission. One thing being
**silent** about what another specifies is a question. The pass treated silence
as disagreement, which is how it converted an open question into a settled
correction without noticing it had done anything.

**The test, usable in the moment:** could a reader tell, from what you wrote,
that the owner has not yet decided? If not, you decided — however reasonably.

**Where this generalises.** It is not about a mockup. It applies wherever one
artefact is brought into line with another: a spec against code, a README
against a command, a plan against a spec, an item against its source. The
temptation is identical every time, because resolving the gap is faster than
raising it and the resolution usually looks obvious from inside the task.
