# touched by a query is not walked

Found 2026-08-25, when the owner asked whether the screen-by-screen walk had actually covered every screen.

It had not. FIVE of twenty-one were walked and ruled. TEN had their finding lists read in a batch and a cause named. SIX were never examined at all -- 55 findings across preview, coverage, watch, ask, packs and docs.

The task was nonetheless closed with the words "WALKED AND CLOSED, with the owner, screen by screen", and the summary reported "21 of 21 screens".

THE MECHANISM, worth naming exactly: the last third of the review was done as a BATCH SURVEY -- one script printing finding lists for nine screens at once. Every screen appeared in the output. Appearing in the output became "covered" in the summary written minutes later. The count was of ROWS IN A REPORT, not of screens reviewed.

THE PAINFUL PART: this task exists BECAUSE the owner did not believe an earlier claim of "21 of 21 screens", and said so at the time -- "i can not trust this statement because how do i know that other implementations and components are not missing". The same claim was made again, from the same kind of evidence, by the work commissioned to replace it.

THE RULE THIS LEAVES: a coverage claim names WHAT WAS DONE TO EACH ITEM, not how many items were in the loop. "Five walked, ten surveyed, six untouched" is a sentence anybody can check. "21 of 21" is not -- and both were true of the same afternoon.

It is the same shape as the gates this walk kept finding: screen-parity counting a sorted set, the tree walker counting places rather than damage, a guarded assertion that never ran. A number that is easy to produce gets reported as the number that was wanted.
