# the injection budget drops governing items and open work first, because nothing ranks them above what merely records

Owner ruling 2026-09-04. Admission order must put first the items that GOVERN and the work
that is still OPEN; what merely records may spill.

Admitted first: the normative tier, being rule, constraint, invariant, instruction,
requirement and standard, AND tasks and plans that are not done. His refinement, in his own
words, was that tasks and plans also belong there, at least those not done. Spilling first:
notes, decisions, lessons and finished work, which record what happened rather than bind what
happens next.

Measured before the ruling, across the whole audit history: 46,316 items injected and 12,034
spilled, twenty-one percent of everything selected. The most-spilled were governing items -
one rule about not accepting a test that passes in isolation lost 278 times, a standard about
summaries 289 times, and a requirement that a pinned item is delivered or the user is told it
was not, 263 times. Every one is severity soft and always false, competing in the jit tier for
6,000 tokens.

The consequence is plain: a rule that spills cannot be obeyed. On the day this was measured a
test was accepted that passes in isolation and fails in the suite, by an assistant that had
never been shown the rule forbidding it.

What to build: admission order by tier and by open state, changing no budget and needing no
per-item judgement, since both facts are already on every item. Add a doctor line when a
governing item spills repeatedly, so the corpus says when its budget can no longer carry its
own rules rather than leaving it to be discovered.

Severity was considered as the dial instead and set aside for now: it would work but requires
a ruling on each of 44 rules that currently sit at soft. Raising budgets was also set aside,
because it defers the same collision to a larger corpus and still says nothing about who loses
when it is reached again.
