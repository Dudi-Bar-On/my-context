# nothing says what v2.0 is, so the open work is a backlog rather than a distance to a release

Measured 2026-09-04. The corpus holds 441 done tasks and 120 that are not: 116 todo, 3 blocked,
one doing. No requirement, plan or non_goal item says what v2.0 includes, and a search for a
release definition returns nothing. The handover document opens by saying we are mid-decision,
keep deciding.

So the question of how far away the release is cannot be answered. 120 open tasks is a backlog
and not a distance, because nothing says which of them are in.

Two facts make this urgent rather than tidy. 46 of the 120 belong to the screen walkthrough, and
by the owner’s own rule those close when he says so rather than when a gate passes, so a third
of the backlog cannot be burned down by any amount of lane work. And on the day this was
measured nine tasks were opened and eight closed, so the list is not converging, because there
is no line for it to converge on.

What to produce: a proposal that puts every open task in or out of v2.0, with a reason, for the
owner to approve or overturn. A proposal and not a mutation. Nothing is marked non_goal and no
item is edited until he has ruled, because retiring work is his call and a wrong cut is harder
to undo than to make.

Suggested principle, to be stated and applied consistently rather than case by case. In: work
without which the product misleads a reader, loses data, or leaves a seam where something is
half built. Out: an enhancement, a second surface for something that already works once, and
anything resting on taste the owner has not ruled on. A task that cannot be placed by that
principle is not guessed at; it is listed for him separately.

The walkthrough items should be counted apart from the rest whatever the cut, since they are
gated on a person rather than on work, and mixing them into one number is what makes the current
figure meaningless.
