# the mockup rewords down to the shipped plain English, rather than the English gaining emphasis

Owner ruling 2026-09-05, walk/16, taken in the screen walkthrough.

THE DISAGREEMENT. preview.whyn ships as plainer English than the mockup’s Hebrew, which carries an
emphasis the English never had. One of the two had to move.

WHAT WAS RULED: the mockup rewords down. The shipped sentence is the better sentence and it stays
as it is; the mockup’s Hebrew loses the emphasis so the two agree at the plainer register.

WHY THIS IS NOT THE MOCKUP LOSING ITS AUTHORITY. INSTR-the-mockup-is-the-ui-specification stands:
the mockup is still what the UI is built to. What this settles is the direction a DISAGREEMENT is
resolved when the shipped text is plainer, and it points at the standard already on the record —
STD-a-screen-explains-itself-in-plain-words-and-depth-hides. A specification that specifies more
ornament than the product needs is corrected toward the product, not obeyed into it.

THE SCOPE OF THIS IS ONE SENTENCE, deliberately. It is not a licence to edit the mockup whenever
shipped text differs — that inverts the instruction above and would make the specification follow
the code. It applies where the only difference is register, the shipped words are plainer, and
both say the same thing.

SEPARATELY, AND AS A FACT RATHER THAN A RULING: work.diffn says "word level" where the diff is
computed at line level. That is wrong rather than ornate, so it is corrected on the same pass.
