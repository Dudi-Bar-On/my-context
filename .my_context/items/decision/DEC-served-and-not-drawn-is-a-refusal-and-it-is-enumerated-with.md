# served and not drawn is a refusal, and it is enumerated with the rest

OWNER RULING, 2026-08-25, walking packs.

Eleven of the twenty-one screens hold data the server COMPUTES and the design of record has no place for. The screens leave it unread rather than inventing a card, and they are RIGHT to -- inventing a card the mockup does not have is what RULE-1-1-with-the-mockup-and-the-owner-says-when-it-is-done forbids.

THE RULING: this is a REFUSAL. A screen declining to draw something it was handed, because the specification gives it nowhere to go, is the same act as a screen declining to draw a chart no endpoint can feed. It goes into the same enumeration, plan:walk seq:12, and is ruled on from ONE list.

NOT RULED YET, deliberately: what happens to any individual field. Three answers were on the table -- give each one a place in the mockup, stop serving what is not drawn, or keep the practice and gate it -- and all three were declined FOR NOW on the same ground: nobody has seen the list. Ruling on eleven screens worth of fields sight-unseen is how a rule gets made that half the instances do not fit.

WHAT THE LIST WILL HOLD, from the two headers read so far: packs leaves `carries[].refusals` unread (each refusing key carries its own sentences, and "four paragraphs of untranslated server English under a two-column table would be a card the design of record does not have"), plus `artefact.protocol`, `artefact.manifest` and `artefact.meaning` which have "no row in `pk.man` to sit in". status.js records the same call for EIGHT served fields. Two screens, eleven fields, and nine screens unread.

THE COST OF LEAVING IT AS IT IS, named because the ruling defers rather than settles: the server knows things no user can see, and until the list exists nobody can say whether that is a considered silence or an accumulated one.
