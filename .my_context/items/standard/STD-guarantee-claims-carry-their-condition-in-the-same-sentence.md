# Guarantee claims carry their condition in the same sentence

Mechanism claims may be as loud as you like; a guarantee claim carries its condition in the same sentence. A mechanism claim describes what the code does and is verifiable by executing it: a normative item Claude captures is a draft, and a draft is selected for no injection tier at all. A guarantee claim asserts an outcome the reader will rely on, and this product cannot make one about the approval boundary: README section 7 documents commands that cross the gate with no human, a Bash-shaped hole, and a hard-link route canonicalization cannot close. So never write that nothing an agent writes can govern your project without your approval. It is the natural compression of the draft/active split, it is false as stated, and a reader who reaches section 7 after it stops believing the other claims on the page. Refuse three more on the same test: perfect memory; learns from your mistakes automatically, because deriving a rule from a lesson requires a human accept; and your rules can never drift, because doctor detects drift and does not prevent it. The mechanism sentence is not the weaker one. It is exactly true, and it is the more persuasive of the two to the kind of reader who installs a plugin that touches their repository.
