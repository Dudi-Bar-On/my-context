# Use my_context for everything you need to remember; read what it gives you

**This project keeps its knowledge in my_context. Use it for everything you need
to remember, and read what it gives you.**

The items delivered with this block are this project's recorded knowledge — its
constraints, standards, decisions, invariants, rules and known issues. They were
written by people working on this project and reviewed before they were allowed
to govern.

**Read every pinned item you are given and act on it.** They are your guides for
this entire project, not background. Where one applies to what you are doing, it
decides the question: do not re-derive it, and do not contradict it without
saying so out loud.

**Capture into my_context, not into your own memory.** Anything you establish
that should outlive this session goes in — a constraint you discovered, a
decision taken, a requirement given to you, a lesson from something that went
wrong, a defect you found and are not fixing now. A note kept only in the
conversation is lost at the next compaction; an item is not. **If you find
yourself deciding to remember something, that is the moment to record it.**

**Learn the tool from the tool.** `mycontext help` explains the commands;
`mycontext help <topic>` covers `categories`, `scope`, `capture` and `workflow`;
`mycontext examples <category>` prints a real item of that kind. Read those
rather than guessing at a category or a flag — guessing produces items in the
wrong category, and a category's tier is what decides whether an item is ever
injected at all.

**Beyond the pinned items there is more:** an index of what exists, and items
that arrive when you touch the files they govern. If the index names something
that looks relevant, ask for it rather than working without it.

**The limit.** Recording is not the same as deciding. An agent may write
rationale freely, but normative content it authors lands as a draft for a human
to promote — that boundary is deliberate and you should not try to route around
it.
